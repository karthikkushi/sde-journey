## What it is

At the end of most interviews you get "Do you have any questions for me?". Good questions show interest and help **you** decide whether you want the job.
"No questions" wastes the chance; questions whose answers are on the website look careless.

## Real-life example

Before renting a flat you ask about water supply, the neighbours and the maintenance charges, not just the rent. The answers decide whether you will be happy there. Interview questions do the same for your next two or three years.

## Picture

```text
 who you're talking to         ask about
 ─────────────────────         ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 engineer (coding round)       daily work, code review, testing, on-call, tech they like or dislike
 manager                       expectations for the first 6 months, how growth/promotion works, team goals
 recruiter / HR                process steps, timeline, level, compensation structure, notice period
 any                           "What do you enjoy most here?"  "What is hard about this team right now?"
```

## How it works inside

- **Prepare 2–3 per interviewer**, matched to their role (see the picture).
- **Strong examples**:
  "What does a good first 90 days look like for this role?" ·
  "How are code reviews and testing done on the team?" ·
  "How do new engineers learn the system? Is there mentoring?" ·
  "What is the biggest technical challenge the team faces this year?" ·
  "How is on-call handled?" ·
  "What did the last person who grew fast here do well?"
- **Listen and follow up**: one good follow-up beats five prepared questions read out.
- **Leave money talk to the recruiter** stage, not the engineer interviews.
- **Write down the answers** after the call; they help compare offers.

## Time and space

| Situation | How many questions |
| --- | --- |
| 5 minutes left | 1–2 good ones |
| 10+ minutes left | 2–3, with follow-ups |
| Interviewer runs out of time | one quick question, thank them |

## Common mistakes

- "No, I don't have any questions."
- Asking things that are on the careers page.
- Asking about salary or leave in a technical round.
- Long questions that are really speeches about yourself.
- Not listening to the answer.

## Patterns that use it

Thinking aloud and STAR (clear, short communication); salary talk (the recruiter stage).

## Practice

```practice
Write 3 questions each for an engineer, a manager and a recruiter.
For a company you are applying to, read their engineering blog and write one question based on it.
In your next mock interview, ask 2 questions and one follow-up.
```

## Explain it back

```explain
Why do your questions at the end matter?
Which questions fit an engineer, and which fit a manager?
Why keep salary questions for the recruiter?
```

## Flashcards

```flashcards
"Any questions?" best answer? :: 2–3 prepared questions suited to the interviewer, plus a follow-up.
Good manager question? :: "What does a good first 90 days look like?"
Good engineer question? :: "How do code review and testing work on your team?"
Salary questions go to? :: The recruiter, not technical interviewers.
```
